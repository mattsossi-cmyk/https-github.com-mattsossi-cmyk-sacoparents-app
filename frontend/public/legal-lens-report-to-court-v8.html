WHY THESE PATHWAYS ARE RELEVANT TO THE ISSUES IDENTIFIED

Communication-based conflict is the primary driver, so structured written communication through the SA CoParents Communication Coach comes first; parent facilitation (Kelly Bernstein) resolves day-to-day bumps while an educational consultation (Kassandra Levay) settles the homework question with facts before any mediation.

Guidance by Type of Conflict

• Communication-based conflict: Consider structured written communication using BIFF principles, a parenting communication platform, or the SA CoParents Communication Coach before escalating the dispute.

• Defined parenting disagreement: Consider child-focused mediation focused on the specific unresolved parenting issue.

• Educational, medical, or psychological disagreement: Consider consultation with an appropriate subject-matter professional followed by mediation or another structured decision-making process.

• Persistent high conflict: Consider a more structured professional process, including parent facilitation, parenting coordination, attorney-assisted negotiation, or mediation.

• Safety or power-imbalance concerns: Conflict-resolution recommendations should be considered separately and with safeguards appropriate to the circumstances.

Safety Override — Family Violence / Safety Considerations

Family-violence or safety concerns should not be treated simply as a higher level of ordinary co-parenting conflict. Where identified, the case should follow the applicable SA CoParents safety assessment and safeguarding pathway.

The rating does not automatically determine the process. Safety screening, professional judgment, court orders, and the specific needs of the child remain controlling considerations.
