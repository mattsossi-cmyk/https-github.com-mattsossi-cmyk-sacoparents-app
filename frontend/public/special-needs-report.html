Purpose

This assessment is designed to help parents identify the unique needs of a child with developmental, medical, behavioral, emotional, educational, or neurodivergent differences. The goal is to improve co-parenting decisions by keeping the child’s needs at the center of every conversation.

Important notice

This report is educational and informational only. It is not a psychological evaluation, medical diagnosis, or legal opinion. Parents are encouraged to consult qualified medical, educational, therapeutic, and legal professionals when appropriate.

Child Strengths

  • Strong relationship with both parents.
  • Responds well to routines.
  • Enjoys visual schedules.
  • Positive relationship with teachers.
  • Benefits from praise and encouragement.
  • Shows resilience during transitions when prepared.

Areas Needing Additional Support

Communication Between Parents

Parents reported differing opinions regarding therapy goals, school communication, behavioral expectations, homework routines, and transitions between homes.

Recommendation: Create one shared communication plan focused only on the child’s needs.

Medical Decision-Making

  • Attend evaluations together whenever appropriate.
  • Share medical reports promptly.
  • Maintain one shared medical calendar.

Educational Collaboration

Both parents should receive school communications and jointly review educational goals, accommodations, and IEP-related information.

Sensory and Emotional Regulation

  • Quiet environments.
  • Transition warnings.
  • Sensory breaks.
  • Visual supports.
  • Predictable routines.

Child-Centered Priorities

  • Emotional safety.
  • Educational success.
  • Stable routines.
  • Physical health.
  • Positive relationships with both parents.
  • Continued therapy.
  • Consistent expectations.

Recommendations

Immediate
  • Share therapy schedules.
  • Develop one transition routine.
  • Create a shared medical notebook or secure app.
  • Establish consistent bedtime expectations.
Next 30 days
  • Attend one medical or therapy appointment together when appropriate.
  • Review educational accommodations.
  • Develop a shared list of calming strategies.
  • Create emergency contact procedures.
Long-term

Develop a written Child Support Plan addressing medical care, therapy goals, school accommodations, communication guidelines, crisis planning, transitions, and future developmental goals.
