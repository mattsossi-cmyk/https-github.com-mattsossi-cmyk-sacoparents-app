A child-focused reflection on your family’s strengths and next steps

Thank you for completing the SA CoParents™ Special Needs Co-Parenting Assessment.

Every child is unique. Children with developmental, medical, behavioral, learning, or emotional differences often benefit most when the important adults in their lives provide consistent support, predictable routines, and coordinated decision-making.

This report is designed to help you identify your family’s strengths, recognize opportunities for growth, and focus on practical strategies that support your child’s well-being. It is not intended to diagnose any medical, psychological, educational, or developmental condition. Rather, it offers guidance to help co-parents work together more effectively while keeping the child’s needs at the center of every decision.

Opportunities for Growth

The assessment identified several areas that may benefit from additional attention.

Communication

Continue sharing important information regarding:

  • school progress
  • appointments
  • medications
  • behavioral changes
  • successes and concerns

Consistency

Children often benefit from similar expectations regarding:

  • bedtime
  • homework
  • routines
  • screen time
  • behavior expectations

Consistency does not require identical households. Rather, it provides children with predictability and reduces unnecessary stress.

School Collaboration

Continue working together by:

  • attending school meetings whenever possible
  • reviewing teacher recommendations
  • supporting homework routines
  • celebrating academic growth
  • communicating respectfully with school personnel

Medical and Therapy Coordination

If your child receives professional services, both parents should strive to:

  • understand treatment goals
  • reinforce therapeutic strategies at home
  • share important updates
  • communicate changes promptly

Developmental & Educational Readiness

Your responses indicate opportunities to continue supporting your child’s development through:

  • language development
  • emotional regulation
  • social relationships
  • independence
  • executive functioning
  • confidence
  • classroom success

Small, consistent efforts from both parents often produce meaningful long-term growth.

Child Impact Reflection

Children often experience greater confidence and emotional security when they know both parents are working toward the same goals.

Even when parents disagree with one another, children generally benefit when adults:

  • reduce conflict
  • communicate respectfully
  • support school success
  • reinforce therapeutic goals
  • maintain predictable routines
  • encourage healthy relationships

Personalized Recommendations

Based upon your assessment, consider focusing on the following priorities during the next several months.

1
Priority One
Develop one shared calendar for school, therapy, and medical appointments.
2
Priority Two
Create consistent routines for homework, bedtime, and transitions between homes.
3
Priority Three
Share important information regarding your child’s progress on a regular basis.
4
Priority Four
Review your child’s educational and therapeutic goals together several times each year.
5
Priority Five
Continue making decisions by asking: “How will this decision help meet our child’s needs?”

Building Your Child’s Foundation

Healthy co-parenting begins by creating a shared understanding of what your child needs most.

The next step in the SA CoParents™ process is to complete your Foundation Statement, where you will identify:

  • your child’s strengths
  • your child’s unique needs
  • shared goals
  • important concerns
  • parenting priorities
  • hopes for the future

This statement becomes the foundation for productive conversations, mediation, and future co-parenting decisions.

Important reminder

Every child develops differently. This report should not be used as a substitute for medical, psychological, educational, or legal advice. If you have concerns regarding your child’s development, learning, behavior, or safety, consult the appropriate healthcare provider, educational professional, therapist, or attorney.

Final Reflection

Your child does not need perfect parents.

Your child benefits most from parents who continue learning, adapting, and working together to provide stability, encouragement, and opportunities for growth.

Every positive step you take toward cooperation strengthens your child’s foundation for success.
