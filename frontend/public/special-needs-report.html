Practical Focus Areas

Where your day-to-day support of a child with additional needs actually happens. Each subsection has a rating and specific next actions.

Pick-Up & Drop-Off (Sensory-Aware Transitions)

Needs Improvement
Your child shows anxiety before exchanges. Regulation drops after transitions, especially when supports don’t travel with her.
  • Consider a neutral, low-stimulation exchange location — school parking lot, familiar quiet park, or library entrance — instead of the front door.
  • Use a visual schedule / transition countdown at both homes 24 hours before each exchange.
  • Ensure comfort items (weighted blanket, headphones, favorite toy) always travel with your child.
  • Establish a scripted, predictable hand-off routine your child can rely on.
  • If safety is ever a concern, most cities offer designated “safe exchange” locations at police stations.

Meeting Your Child’s Educational Needs (IEP / 504)

Fair
You attend ARD/IEP meetings and reinforce accommodations at home. The other parent isn’t reliably included in IEP updates, 504 changes, or classroom behavior plans.
  • Add both parents to the school’s parent portal, teacher email lists, and special-education case-manager updates.
  • Attend IEP/ARD/504 meetings together when possible; if not, send a written summary within 48 hours.
  • Align classroom accommodations (visual supports, sensory breaks, extended time) with home routines.
  • Share progress-monitoring data, behavior plans, and executive-function goals in one central document.
  • Coordinate on tutoring, therapy pull-outs, and adaptive extracurricular sign-ups.

Medical, Therapy & Skill Reinforcement

Good
You coordinate specialists, therapy appointments, and medications well. Therapy carryover at the other parent’s home is inconsistent, which slows progress.
  • Maintain one shared medical & therapy calendar (AppClose, OFW, or a shared Google Calendar).
  • Attend annual well-checks, specialist evaluations, and IEP-related medical letters together when possible.
  • Share a one-page therapy homework sheet from OT, speech, and behavioral providers so both homes reinforce the same skills.
  • Use identical medication routines — same time, same dose, same tracker — in both homes.
  • Agree on an emergency contact protocol: who to call first, in what order, and how quickly.

More Practical Focus Areas

Special Events at School

Fair
You attend most classroom performances and awards, but sensory-heavy events (Field Day, holiday parties) can overwhelm your child without coordinated supports.
  • Share the school-year calendar with the other parent each semester — conferences, IEP meetings, performances, field trips.
  • Before large or sensory-heavy events, coordinate sensory supports (headphones, quiet area, exit plan) with the teacher and the other parent.
  • Confirm with your child which parent will attend so she isn’t caught in scheduling ambiguity.
  • Attend IEP/ARD/504 progress-review meetings together when possible.
  • Celebrate promotions, awards, and small wins in both homes.

Extracurricular & Adaptive Activities

Fair
Your child does adaptive swimming and a social-skills group. Attendance during your time is consistent; you’re less certain about the other parent’s time.
  • Share the season/term activity schedule with the other parent as early as possible.
  • Coordinate transportation and equipment hand-offs; make sure sensory supports, communication devices, and comfort items travel with her.
  • Support the same activity during both parenting times — inconsistent attendance erodes skill progress and social confidence.
  • Give coaches, instructors, and social-skills leaders a single point of contact and share her strengths and support needs proactively.
  • Consider therapy-based activities (therapeutic riding, adaptive recreation, sensory play groups) that reinforce OT/speech goals.
SA CoParents™ communication toolkit

Because communication is your priority focus area

Two evidence-based tools are recommended when co-parent communication is flagged as Needs Improvement. Both are widely used in family courts, by mediators, and by special-education case managers.
B
Brief
Keep messages to 3–4 sentences. Long replies invite escalation.
I
Informative
Facts only — therapy times, medication doses, IEP updates. No interpretation.
F
Friendly
Polite tone, no sarcasm. Assume it will be read in court.
F
Firm
Clear about what you need. Close the loop; don’t invite further debate.
AppClose
Free · iOS & Android
Shared calendar, secure messaging, expense tracking, and swap requests. Message history is court-admissible. A strong first step when cost is a barrier.
OurFamilyWizard (OFW)
Subscription · Court-mandated in many states
Calendar, messaging with ToneMeter™ that flags emotional language before you send, expense ledger, and journal. Widely accepted by family courts as an evidentiary record.

SA CoParents™ distinguishing feature

Child Impact Predictor

Rather than identifying areas of disagreement alone, the Child Impact Predictor estimates how differences between households may affect your child in practical, day-to-day areas — especially important when your child has additional support needs. This shifts the focus away from blaming the other parent and toward understanding how specific co-parenting patterns influence your child’s regulation, therapy progress, and school success.

Personalized Recommendations

Adopt the BIFF method for every co-parenting message this month.
Choose one shared calendar/messaging app (AppClose or OFW).
Build a written IEP/504 summary and share within 48 hours of every update.
Create a one-page “how my child regulates” sheet for both homes and providers.

Book a child-focused mediation session with a mediator experienced in special-needs cases.
Add both parents to therapy carryover updates from OT, speech, and behavioral providers.
Consult a special-needs family attorney if any issue remains unresolved after 60 days.

Foundation statement

My commitment to my child

“My child benefits from predictable routines, coordinated medical and therapeutic care, consistent educational support, and calm communication with the other parent. I recognize that although the other parent and I may see things differently, my responsibility is to provide stability, reduce unnecessary conflict, and support my child’s developmental, emotional, and educational needs. I commit to making decisions that prioritize my child’s well-being and to seeking professional guidance whenever needed.”

Important reminder

Every child develops differently. This report is educational and informational only. It is not a psychological evaluation, medical diagnosis, therapy, or legal advice. If you have concerns regarding your child’s development, learning, behavior, or safety, consult the appropriate healthcare provider, educational professional, therapist, or attorney. If you are in crisis, call 911 or the National Domestic Violence Hotline at 1-800-799-7233.

Every positive step you take toward cooperation strengthens your child’s foundation for success.
