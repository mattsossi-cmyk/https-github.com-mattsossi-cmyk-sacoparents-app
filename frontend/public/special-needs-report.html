Thank you for completing the SA CoParents™ Special Needs Co-Parenting Assessment.

This report reflects your responses about how your family currently supports your child’s needs. It highlights your strengths, identifies where you can grow, and gives you practical strategies to try — whether or not the other parent participates yet. It is not intended to diagnose any medical, psychological, educational, or developmental condition. It offers guidance to help you keep your child’s needs at the center of every decision.

Assessment Findings by Section

Each of the eight sections is summarized below with its own rating and a brief finding.

Section 1
Understanding Your Child
Excellent
You clearly understand your child’s diagnosis and can describe her core strengths and challenges. Based on your responses, you and the other parent share a similar developmental picture.
Section 2
Medical & Therapeutic Care
Good
You attend therapy consistently and stay informed about appointments. You may be carrying more of the current treatment-plan detail than the other parent — sharing a written summary would help even the load.
Section 3
Educational Support
Fair
You receive most school communications and follow up on your child’s academic progress. You attend ARD/IEP meetings more often than the other parent, and homework expectations differ between the two homes.
Section 4
Daily Living Consistency
Good
In your home, meals, hygiene, and homework expectations are consistent and predictable. Bedtime, screen-time limits, and transition routines still differ from the other home.
Section 5
Communication Between Parents
Needs Improvement
You’d like to share medications, sleep patterns, and behavioral changes more proactively with the other parent, but there is no shared calendar or communication app in place. Information often flows only after a problem escalates.
Section 6
Child Regulation & Transitions
Fair
You notice that your child shows anxiety before exchanges. You use visual schedules and sensory supports at home, but you’re unsure whether the other parent does — your child experiences a noticeable regulation drop after transitions.
Section 7
Parent Collaboration Readiness
Needs Improvement
Most disagreements between you and the other parent are about day-to-day parenting decisions rather than the diagnosis itself. Structured mediation or child-focused coaching is likely to help you move from reactive conversations to planned ones.
Section 8
Future Planning
Fair
You have a positive long-term vision for your child, but decisions around middle-school placement, guardianship, adult services, and financial planning have not yet been documented or discussed with the other parent.

Your Strengths

  • You clearly recognize your child’s diagnosis and needs.
  • You maintain consistent therapy attendance.
  • Your long-term goals for your child are stable and child-centered.
  • Your home actively supports your child’s educational success.

Areas to Improve

  • Propose a shared therapy calendar to the other parent.
  • Advocate for consistent bedtime expectations across both homes.
  • Establish a written channel for medication updates.
  • Create a transition routine you use before every exchange.
  • Prepare with the other parent before annual IEP meetings.

SA CoParents™ distinguishing feature

Child Impact Predictor

Rather than identifying areas of disagreement alone, the Child Impact Predictor estimates how differences between households may affect your child in practical, day-to-day areas. This shifts the focus away from blaming the other parent and toward understanding how specific co-parenting patterns influence the child’s lived experience — consistent with SA CoParents™’ guiding message.

Child Needs Foundation Statement

Instead of ending on a numeric score, the SA CoParents™ assessment closes with your personal, child-centered statement — something you can return to during future decisions and mediation, regardless of whether the other parent participates.

Foundation statement

My commitment to my child

“My child benefits from predictable routines, coordinated medical care, consistent educational support, and calm communication with the other parent. I recognize that although our parenting styles may differ, my responsibility is to provide stability, reduce unnecessary conflict, and support my child’s developmental, emotional, and educational needs. I commit to making decisions that prioritize my child’s well-being and to seeking professional guidance whenever needed.”

What Happens Next in the SA CoParents App

  • Review this assessment and save it to your dashboard.
  • Complete your personalized Foundation Statement.
  • Build your Child Needs Statement for mediation.
  • Create your Co-Parenting Commitment.
  • Prepare shared materials for mediation, IEP, or medical meetings.
  • Reassess after significant changes in your child’s needs.
Important reminder

Every child develops differently. This report is educational and informational only. It is not a psychological evaluation, medical diagnosis, therapy, or legal advice. If you have concerns regarding your child’s development, learning, behavior, or safety, consult the appropriate healthcare provider, educational professional, therapist, or attorney.

Every positive step you take toward cooperation strengthens your child’s foundation for success.
