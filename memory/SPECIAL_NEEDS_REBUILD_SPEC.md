## 1. Assessment framework (verbatim from user)

Special Needs Coparenting Assessment
Every child deserves consistent support across both homes. When a child has developmental, medical, behavioral, learning, sensory, or emotional needs, differences between parents can make coordination more difficult.
This assessment helps you:
 Describe your child's individual strengths and needs.
 Identify areas of agreement and concern.
 Evaluate consistency between homes.
 Prepare for conversations with educators and healthcare professionals.
 Develop practical options for a child-focused parenting plan.
This is an educational and planning tool. It does not diagnose a child, determine parental fitness, replace a professional evaluation, or make legal recommendations.

### Section 1: About Your Child (intake / unscored)
- Child's age and current grade.
- What are your child's greatest strengths?
- What activities, interests, or relationships bring your child joy?
- Has your child received a diagnosis or professional evaluation?
  - No diagnosis or evaluation
  - Evaluation is being considered
  - Evaluation is scheduled
  - Evaluation is in progress
  - Diagnosis or eligibility determination received
  - Prefer not to answer
- Which needs currently apply? (multi-select)
  Learning or academic · Speech or language · Autism-related · ADHD or executive functioning · Intellectual or developmental · Physical or mobility · Chronic medical · Sensory processing · Behavioral · Anxiety or emotional regulation · Trauma-related · Social development · Sleep · Feeding or nutrition · Other
- Briefly describe your understanding of your child's needs.

### Section 2: Understanding and Acceptance (Likert 1–5 + N/A)
Scale: 1 Strongly disagree · 2 Disagree · 3 Unsure · 4 Agree · 5 Strongly agree · N/A Not applicable or unknown

Items:
1. I understand my child's diagnosis, suspected condition, or areas of concern.
2. I recognize how my child's needs affect daily life.
3. I can separate my concerns about the other parent from my child's actual needs.
4. I am willing to consider information that differs from my current understanding.
5. I rely on qualified professionals when medical, educational, or developmental questions arise.
6. I understand that my child may behave differently in each home.
7. I believe the other parent's observations should be considered even when they differ from mine.
8. Both parents generally acknowledge that our child may need additional support.

### Section 3: Medical and Mental Healthcare (multi-select intake + 9 Likert + 3 open)
Providers currently in care (multi-select): Pediatrician · Medical specialist · Psychiatrist · Psychologist · Counselor or therapist · Occupational therapist · Physical therapist · Speech-language pathologist · Behavioral specialist · Other provider

Likert items:
1. I know the names and contact information of my child's providers.
2. I receive timely information about appointments and recommendations.
3. Both parents can access appropriate medical records.
4. Recommended appointments are scheduled and attended.
5. Medications are administered consistently and as prescribed.
6. Medication changes and side effects are promptly shared.
7. Therapy exercises or treatment plans are followed in my home.
8. I understand what to do during a medical or behavioral emergency.
9. The parents have a workable process for resolving disagreements about treatment.

Open-ended:
- What medical or mental-health needs require the greatest coordination?
- Have appointments, medication, therapy, or treatment ever been delayed or missed? Explain.
- What would improve consistency between homes?

### Section 4: Kindergarten and Elementary School (intake + 10 Likert + 3 open)
Intake:
- School name
- Which of these apply to your child? (multi-select) IEP · Section 504 Plan · RtI/MTSS support · Behavior Intervention Plan · Individualized Health Plan · Classroom accommodations · Informal teacher supports · Evaluation pending · None · Unsure

Likert:
1. I understand my child's educational plan and accommodations.
2. I know who to contact at the school when concerns arise.
3. Both parents receive school notices, progress reports, and teacher communications.
4. Both parents attend or meaningfully participate in school meetings when appropriate.
5. Homework and reading expectations are reasonably consistent between homes.
6. School supplies, technology, medication, and assistive devices travel reliably between homes.
7. Attendance and morning routines support my child's learning.
8. Both parents follow the child's educational plan.
9. Teachers receive consistent and accurate information from the parents.
10. Educational disagreements are handled without placing the child or school staff in the middle.

Open-ended:
- What educational support is most important for your child?
- What school-related problem occurs most often?
- What changes would help your child succeed?

### Section 5: Transitions, Pickup, and Drop-Off (10 Likert + 3 open)
1. My child understands the parenting-time schedule.
2. Pickup and drop-off times are predictable.
3. Necessary medication, schoolwork, clothing, comfort items, and equipment accompany the child.
4. Schedule changes are communicated early enough to support the child.
5. Exchanges occur without exposing the child to conflict.
6. Each parent understands the child's transition-related triggers.
7. The child receives appropriate preparation before changing homes.
8. Both homes support calming or decompression time after transitions.
9. Transportation arrangements accommodate the child's physical, sensory, or behavioral needs.
10. The parents have a backup plan when transportation or caregiving arrangements fail.

Open-ended:
- What makes transitions difficult for your child?
- Which items must always travel with your child?
- Would a written transition checklist help?

### Section 6: Daily Routines Across Homes (consistency scale, 12 items + 3 open)
Response options: Consistent · Mostly consistent · Sometimes inconsistent · Frequently inconsistent · Unknown · Not applicable

Items: Sleep and bedtime · Morning routine · Medication · Meals and nutrition · Hygiene · Homework and reading · Screen time · Sensory supports · Behavioral expectations · Therapy exercises · Assistive devices · Emotional-regulation strategies

Open-ended:
- Which routines must remain consistent for your child to function well?
- Where is flexibility appropriate?
- Which differences between homes appear to negatively affect the child?

### Section 7: Communication and Information Sharing (8 Likert + preferred method multi-select)
1. Parents exchange child-related information accurately and promptly.
2. Communications focus on the child rather than past relationship conflict.
3. Each parent provides updates after significant appointments or school meetings.
4. Both parents can ask reasonable questions without the conversation escalating.
5. Important decisions are confirmed in writing.
6. Parents avoid using the child as a messenger.
7. Professionals are not asked to take sides in parental disputes.
8. A structured communication method would improve coordination.

Preferred communication structure (multi-select): Direct conversation · Text message · Email · Co-parenting application · BIFF-style written communication · Communication through attorneys · Communication through a parenting facilitator or coordinator · Information exchanged directly through professionals · Another structured method

### Section 8: Special School Events (context list + 8 Likert)
Context list (informational): Parent-teacher conferences · IEP or Section 504 meetings · School performances · Field trips · Awards ceremonies · Class parties · Testing days · Therapy or intervention meetings · School transitions · Special-education evaluations

Likert items:
1. Both parents receive advance notice of important events.
2. Both parents understand who may attend.
3. The child can participate without worrying about parental conflict.
4. Parents can attend together safely and appropriately when beneficial.
5. Separate attendance or virtual participation is available when needed.
6. Parents avoid discussing disputes during the event.
7. A designated parent shares information when only one parent can attend.
8. School staff understand the appropriate communication arrangement.

### Section 9: Extracurricular Activities (open intake + 10 Likert)
- What activities does your child currently participate in?
- Does the activity support the child's interests, development, or therapeutic needs?

Likert:
1. The activity is appropriate for the child's abilities and tolerance.
2. Both parents receive schedules and contact information.
3. Transportation responsibilities are clear.
4. Equipment and uniforms are available when needed.
5. Costs and payment responsibilities are understood.
6. The activity does not create an unreasonable burden for the child.
7. Coaches or instructors receive necessary medical or accommodation information.
8. Parents behave appropriately when attending at the same time.
9. The child is not pressured to choose which parent attends.
10. There is a process for approving new activities.

### Section 10: Decision-Making and Professional Guidance (7 Likert + decision-support multi-select)
1. Parents can identify when a professional opinion is needed.
2. Decisions are based on the child's needs rather than parental control.
3. Both parents have an opportunity to provide relevant information.
4. Professional recommendations are documented and shared.
5. Parents understand who has legal authority to make particular decisions.
6. There is a workable process for time-sensitive decisions.
7. The child is not asked to decide disputes that belong to the adults.

Decision-support options (multi-select, informational): Joint consultation with pediatrician · Educational meeting with teachers/school specialists · Neutral child therapist · Developmental or neuropsychological evaluation · Parenting facilitator or parenting coordinator · Child-focused mediation · Tie-breaking recommendation from an agreed professional · Medical tie-breaker · Mental-health tie-breaker · Educational tie-breaker · Court clarification when legal authority is disputed
Note: "A professional should not be designated as a binding tie-breaker unless the arrangement is legally permitted and clearly documented."

### Section 11: Family Violence and Communication Safety (UNSCORED — special)
"This section should not produce a general 'co-parenting score.' Safety concerns require an individualized response."

Multi-select: I am afraid of the other parent · Communications include threats, harassment, intimidation, or degrading language · The other parent monitors my location, devices, or accounts · Exchanges have involved threatening or unsafe behavior · The child has witnessed conflict, threats, or violence · A protective order or other safety restriction exists · Direct negotiation or joint mediation may not be safe · The other parent interferes with medical, educational, or therapeutic care as a means of control · None of these apply · Prefer not to answer

Safeguards to display (informational multi-select): BIFF communication · Parenting application with documented messages · Communication limited to child-related information · Separate arrival and departure times · Neutral or supervised exchange location · Third-party or professional exchanges · Information provided directly by schools and medical providers · Shuttle or separate-room mediation · Attorney-assisted negotiation · Separate professional consultations · Safety planning with a qualified advocate · Review by a family-law attorney · Emergency or court intervention when necessary

"A positive safety response should direct the parent to the app's full Family Violence–IPV Communication Safety Assessment."

### Section 12: Parent Capacity and Support (10 Likert + 3 open)
1. I can remain regulated when discussing my child's needs.
2. I can follow detailed care instructions.
3. I have reliable transportation to appointments and school events.
4. I can obtain necessary medication, equipment, and supplies.
5. I have family, community, or professional support.
6. I ask for help when I cannot meet a need.
7. I am willing to learn new caregiving skills.
8. My current stress level sometimes affects my ability to follow the child's plan.
9. Financial or scheduling barriers interfere with services.
10. I need additional training or support.

Open-ended:
- What support would make it easier for you to meet your child's needs?
- What is one caregiving responsibility you believe you manage well?
- What is one area where you need help?

### Section 13: Goals, Concerns, and Priorities (all open-ended)
- List your three most important goals for your child.
- List your three greatest concerns.
- What does your child need most from you?
- What does your child need from the other parent?
- What does your child need both parents to do consistently?
- Which issue should be addressed first?
- What professional information is still needed?
- What agreement could the parents try during the next 30 days?

### Report Scoring Framework
Scores by category (never labeling a parent good or bad).

| Average score | Report classification |
|---|---|
| 4.25–5.00 | Established strength |
| 3.50–4.24 | Generally supported |
| 2.75–3.49 | Coordination opportunity |
| 2.00–2.74 | Significant inconsistency |
| 1.00–1.99 | Priority concern |

Comparison rule: highlight agreement · differences in knowledge · differences in observations · missing information · conflicting priorities · safety concerns (never auto-disclosed) · questions for professionals · topics for child-focused mediation.

Closing statement:
"Your child does not need both homes to be identical. Your child does need each parent to understand the child's needs, follow essential care plans, share important information, and protect the child from adult conflict. Use this report to identify strengths, clarify concerns, and determine where professional guidance or a more detailed agreement may be helpful."

## 2. Report structure (verbatim from user)

The Special Needs Co-Parenting Report should follow the assessment section by section. It should summarize one parent's responses without determining whether that parent is right or wrong. The same format can later be used to compare the other parent's independently generated report.

Every section must follow the pattern:
**response → finding → child impact → suggested option → action step**

### Report cover
Parent completing assessment · Child (first name or initials) · Child's age and grade · Assessment date · Report type: Individual Parent Report · Assessment version.

### Purpose of this report
Educational + planning; not diagnostic, not parental fitness, not legal advice. Framed as "based on this parent's responses."

### 1. Report at a Glance
- Child's identified strengths (up to 3)
- Child's primary needs (up to 3)
- Parent's highest priorities (up to 3)
- Areas of established strength (bulleted)
- Areas needing greater coordination (bulleted)
- Priority concerns (bulleted, may say "No priority concerns identified")
- Professional questions requiring clarification (medical / educational / mental-health or developmental)

### 2. Category Summary (table)
Columns: Assessment category · Score · Classification · Primary finding.
Rows must include every scored category. Safety row: Score = "Not scored"; Classification = "Separate review". Footer note: "Scores reflect the reporting parent's answers and are not findings of fact."

### 3. Child Profile
- Age and grade
- Child's strengths (narrative)
- Child's interests and sources of enjoyment (narrative)
- Diagnoses, suspected conditions, or areas under evaluation
- Identified areas of need (checklist visualisation)
- Report summary paragraph: "According to this parent, the child demonstrates strengths in [strengths]. The parent identifies the child's primary support needs as [needs]. The parent reports that [diagnostic/evaluation status]."

### 4. Understanding and Acceptance
Category score + Classification.
Reported strengths (bulleted).
Areas for development (bulleted).
Parent's perspective (verbatim summary of parent's open answers).
Suggested next steps (bulleted).

### 5. Medical and Mental Healthcare
Current providers table: Provider type · Provider identified? · Parent has contact info? · Coordination concern?
Assessment result (score + classification).
Reported strengths.
Coordination concerns.
Missed or delayed care reported by parent.
Suggested next steps.

### 6. Kindergarten and Elementary School
Current educational supports (IEP/504/BIP/IHP/Accommodations/Evaluation pending).
Assessment result.
Reported strengths.
Educational coordination concerns.
Parent's primary educational concern.
Suggested next steps.

### 7. Transitions, Pickup, and Drop-Off
Assessment result.
Child's reported transition needs (checklist visualisation).
Reported challenges (bulleted).
Essential transition checklist (checkbox visualisation).
Suggested next steps.

### 8. Daily Routines Across Homes (table)
Columns: Routine · Parent's reported consistency · Importance to child · Action needed.
Rows for each Section-6 assessment item.
Report summary paragraph: essential routines vs. flexibility areas.

### 9. Communication and Information Sharing
Assessment result.
Current communication method.
Information reportedly exchanged effectively.
Information-sharing concerns.
Recommended communication structure (bulleted).

### 10. School Events and Extracurricular Activities
School events: Events requiring coordination checklist · Reported concern · Proposed arrangement.
Extracurricular activities table: Activity · Child benefit · Transportation plan · Cost arrangement · Attendance concern.
Suggested next steps.

### 11. Decision-Making and Professional Guidance
Assessment result.
Decisions currently requiring attention (checklist).
Parent's preferred decision-making process.
Professional input that may be helpful (checklist).
Potential resolution structure (bulleted).

### 12. Family Violence and Communication Safety
"This section must be separated from the scored report."
Safety-screen result — one of: No safety concerns disclosed · Communication-safety concerns identified · Additional Family Violence–IPV Assessment recommended · Immediate professional safety review recommended.
Recommended safeguards — display only applicable safeguards.
**"Sensitive safety answers should not automatically appear in a report shared with the other parent."** → In share/comparison views, this whole section is redacted by default.

### 13. Parent Capacity and Support
Assessment result.
Parent-reported strengths (bulleted).
Barriers identified (checklist).
Support requested (narrative).

### 14. Parent's Goals, Concerns, and Priorities
Three primary goals · Three primary concerns · What the child needs from this parent · What the parent believes the child needs from the other parent (labelled as the reporting parent's perspective) · What the child needs from both parents.

### 15. Recommended Child-Focused Action Plan (table)
Columns: Priority · Recommended action · Responsible person · Professional support · Target date.
Recommended 30-day steps (bulleted).

### 16. Questions for the Other Parent or a Professional
Auto-derived neutral questions from unclear or conflicting answers:
- What medication schedule has the provider recommended?
- Which routines are essential in both homes?
- How will each parent receive school notices?
- What items must accompany the child during exchanges?
- Who will schedule and attend appointments?
- How will appointment summaries be shared?
- What should happen when the parents disagree about treatment?
- Which professional is best qualified to answer the disputed question?

### 17. Closing Summary
"Based on this parent's responses, the child's primary strengths include [strengths], and the child's principal support needs involve [needs]. The parent reports established strengths in [categories] and identifies a need for greater coordination in [categories]. The most immediate child-focused priorities are [priorities]. Professional clarification may be helpful concerning [questions]."

Parent reflection:
- One thing I can do now
- One issue that requires cooperation
- One question that requires professional guidance

## 3. Confirmed rebuild decisions (user-approved)

1. **Delivery cadence: Phase A only for the next session.** Deliver backend schema + deterministic scoring + report content builder + pytest coverage. No frontend, no PDF renderer, no invite/comparison in Phase A. Later phases: B frontend assessment flow; C individual PDF + share; D invite + comparison view.

2. **Section 11 (Family Violence) handling — confirmed:**
   - Captured privately on the parent's own assessment record.
   - Kept out of every general co-parenting score (never averaged).
   - Included in the parent's OWN report (Report Section 12).
   - **Redacted by default in every shared / comparison view.**
   - A positive answer surfaces the standalone Family Violence Assessment as the recommended next step.

3. **Multi-select / intake items handled like Section 1 (context, not scored):**
   - Section 3 providers checklist — context.
   - Section 4 school-plan checklist — context.
   - Section 7 preferred communication method — context.
   - Section 8 events context list — context.
   - Section 9 activity intake text + benefit question — context.
   - Section 10 decision-support options — context.
   - Section 11 safeguards options — context.
   - Section 13 (all open-ended) — context.
   - **Section 6 (Daily Routines Across Homes)** — treated as a scored category because the report's Category Summary shows it with a score. Map the consistency scale to a 1–5 numeric so it averages the same way as the Likert sections:
     - Consistent = 5
     - Mostly consistent = 4
     - Sometimes inconsistent = 3
     - Frequently inconsistent = 2
     - Unknown = excluded (like N/A)
     - Not applicable = excluded

   Scored categories are therefore Sections **2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, and 12** (10 categories). Section 11 is safety-only (unscored). Sections 1 and 13 are pure context.

4. **Fresh names (no re-use of `sn_v3` or `special-needs-v3`).** Locked-in naming for Phase A:
   - Backend files: `snc_questions.py`, `snc_scoring.py`, `snc_report_content.py` (+ `snc_routes.py`, `snc_report_pdf.py` in later phases).
   - FastAPI prefix: `/api/snc`.
   - MongoDB collections: `snc_assessments`, `snc_reports`, `snc_invites`.
   - Frontend route (Phase B): `/special-needs`.
   - Shared viewer (Phase C): `/shared/snc/:token`.
   - Invite acceptance (Phase D): `/invites/snc/:token`.
   - `snc` = "Special Needs Co-parenting" — short, disambiguates from the removed v2 (`special_needs_*`) and v3 (`sn_v3_*`) code.

5. **Data-model target for Phase A** (agent may adjust names for clarity but not semantics):
   - `snc_assessments`: `{ user_id, section_1: {…}, section_2_answers: {qid: 1-5|"na"}, …, section_11: {…}, section_13: {…}, completed: bool, created_at, updated_at }`
   - Score output shape (per category):
     `{ id, number, name, avg (float, 1-5), classification (Established strength | Generally supported | Coordination opportunity | Significant inconsistency | Priority concern), answered, total, na_count }`
   - Top-level scored payload:
     `{ categories: [ … 10 rows … ], safety: { flagged: bool, selected_flags: […] }, overall_summary_hint, generated_at }`

## 4. Report content generator — required behaviour
Every report section must follow the pattern **response → finding → child impact → suggested option → action step.** The Phase A `snc_report_content.py` must:
- Populate the Category Summary table for the 10 scored categories.
- Auto-derive Report Section 16 "Questions for the Other Parent or a Professional" from any answer of 1 or 2 (or "Frequently inconsistent" / "Sometimes inconsistent" for Section 6), from any Section 3/10 items marked as concerns, and from any Section 13 goals/concerns that name a professional domain.
- Populate Section 15 Action Plan from the lowest-scoring 2–4 categories.
- Never label a parent right or wrong.
- Redact Section 12 content when `include_safety=False` (shared view).
